## 1. Take good photos

The free preview understands a room as a box. Photos taken the way it expects give the best results:

| Do | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| Stand in the doorway or against the wall opposite the one you photograph | Shooting into a corner (two walls at an angle) |
| Face the far wall **straight on** and hold the phone **level** at chest height | Tilting the phone up or down |
| Use the normal (1×) lens; the 0.5× ultra-wide also works if you select it | Zooming in on details |
| Take photos in daylight, or with work lights on | Pointing at a bright window or the sun |
| Clear tools and rubble from the floor if you can (the app paints over them anyway) | Blurry photos taken while moving |

For the **house exterior**, stand on the street or in the yard facing the front of the house.

## 2. Upload your photos

Drag photos onto **Photos of your space**, or tap it to choose files (on a phone you can take a new photo). You can add up to 12 photos per project. Each one gets a thumbnail and a **room type**: living room, kitchen, bedroom, bathroom or full house exterior. The app guesses the type from the file name ("kitchen.jpg"); change it in the list if needed.

The app checks each photo and explains any problem:

| Message | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| **Too small** | Use the original photo from your phone, not a screenshot or a WhatsApp-compressed copy. |
| **Too dark** | Retake it in daylight or with lights on. |
| **Blurry or unclear** | Hold the phone steady and tap the screen to focus. |
| **Almost one flat colour** | Make sure the camera shows the room, not a wall up close. |
| **HEIC photo** | On iPhone choose Settings › Camera › Formats › *Most Compatible*, or export the photo as JPEG. |

If you still want to try a photo with a problem, press **Use it anyway**.

Just exploring? Press **Try sample photos** to load four example spaces.

## 3. Line up the room box

Press **Adjust room box**. You will see a white rectangle and an orange dot over your photo:

1. Drag the **four white corners** onto the corners of the far wall (where it meets the floor and the ceiling).
2. Drag the **orange dot** to where the floor and ceiling lines would meet if you extended them. For a straight-on photo this is near the middle of the far wall.
3. If you used the ultra-wide lens, choose it under **Lens**.
4. Press **Done**.

The dashed lines should run along the corners where the side walls meet the floor and ceiling. The app shows an estimated room size, for example "4.9 m wide × 5.6 m deep × 3.0 m high". This is a rough estimate that assumes the photo was taken about 1.5 m above the floor.

A well-placed box gives correct perspective for the floor and walls, sensible furniture placement and a better 3D walkthrough. The app tries to find the far wall automatically (**Auto-detect**), but check it.

## 4. Choose a style or add reference images

**Preset styles**, for when you have no reference images:

| Style | Typical look |
| --- | --- |
| Modern minimalist | Warm white walls, large grey porcelain tiles, cove lighting, black details |
| Scandinavian | Soft white walls, light oak floor, sage and oatmeal textiles, plants |
| Luxury contemporary | Marble floor, fluted wood feature wall, brass, deep velvet colours, evening light |
| Indian modern | Terracotta accent wall, patterned cement tiles, teak and cane, brass, mustard and teal |
| Industrial loft | Exposed brick, polished concrete, dark ceiling, black steel, Edison bulbs |

**Reference images** (up to 5 per project). Add a picture you like and choose what it should control:

| Role | Use it for | What it changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Overall style** | A finished room you love | Wall colour, floor type and tone, furniture colours, light warmth |
| **Flooring** | A close-up photo of tiles, wood, stone or concrete | The floor only (the photo becomes the floor texture) |
| **Kitchen** | A kitchen you like | Cabinet colour and worktop |
| **Colour palette** | A palette card, fabric, painting or any colourful image | Wall paint, accent colours, soft furnishings |
| **Furniture** | A sofa, bed or furniture set | Upholstery and wood tones |

Give each reference a short label (for example "Oak sample" or "Aunt's living room"). Your references lead, and the preset fills in whatever they don't cover.

**Warnings.** If a reference doesn't fit the room, the app keeps your photo's layout and tells you what it ignored. For example:

- *"Kitchen idea" is a kitchen reference but this photo is a bedroom, so it is not used here.*
- *"Hotel lobby" looks like a whole room rather than a close-up of the floor material, so only its floor colour is used.*
- *"Garden" looks like an outdoor scene but this is a living room. Only its colours and mood are used.*

The self-hosted version can also run an **AI reference check** (Claude), which spots subtler conflicts such as a double-height room used as the style for a small bedroom.

## 5. Render and compare

Press **Render**. The free preview takes a few seconds and runs on your device.

- **Slider**: drag the handle (or use the arrow keys) to wipe between before and after.
- **Side by side**: before and after next to each other (stacked on phones).
- **After only**: just the finished room.

Under the image you'll see which **references were used** and what each one changed, any **warnings**, and the full list of **finishes** in the render.

Changing the style, a reference, the room type or the room box marks the render as out of date. With the free preview it updates automatically; untick **Update automatically** if you prefer to press **Update render** yourself. AI engines always wait for you to press it, because each render costs money.

## 6. Change one element at a time

Open **Edit one element** and pick an element:

| Element | Options |
| --- | --- |
| Flooring | 14 materials (oak, walnut, herringbone, porcelain, marble, terrazzo, cement tiles, Kota stone…) or your flooring reference |
| Walls & paint | Any paint colour (with quick picks from your references), limewash finish, feature wall (marble, fluted wood, slats, brick, stone, accent paint), wall tiles |
| Ceiling | Flat, cove false ceiling, timber slats or exposed concrete; colour |
| Lighting | Daylight or evening mood, colour temperature (2700–5000 K), downlights, brightness |
| Fixtures | Metal finish (black, brass, chrome…), pendant lights, window frame and door colours, skirting |
| Cabinets & vanity | Cabinet colour, worktop, splashback, handles (kitchens and bathrooms) |
| Furniture | Fabric, accent and wood colours, rug pattern, minimal or cosy amount |

Press **Apply** to re-render with only that element changed. A dot on the tab means you changed it for this photo, and **Reset to style** undoes it. Every version is kept in the strip under the editor, so you can compare options. With an AI engine you can also add a note such as "matte finish, no pattern".

## 7. Save and download

- **Save** keeps a render in the project's **Saved** tab.
- **Download** saves the finished image as a JPEG.
- **Download before/after** saves one labelled image with both photos, ready for WhatsApp or email.

## 8. 3D house and walkthrough video

Open **3D house & video** after rendering at least one room.

- **Walkthrough**: press play to fly up to the house exterior (if you rendered one), see an overview of all rooms, then walk through each room in turn. Drag the bar to scrub.
- **Explore**: drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom.
- **Rooms in the walkthrough**: reorder with the arrows, or untick rooms to leave them out.
- **Download walkthrough MP4**: choose 720p or 1080p and a pace, then wait for the progress bar. Cancel at any time.
- **Download 3D model (.glb)**: opens in Windows 3D Viewer, Blender, or online viewers such as gltf-viewer.donmccurdy.com.

The 3D house is built from your photos: each room is a box textured with its finished render, with 3D furniture from the free preview. It is for getting a feel for the space, not for measurements.

## Projects and privacy

Use the project menu at the top to create, rename, switch between or delete projects, for example one per house or client. Everything is stored **in your browser** on this device. Clearing your browser data deletes projects, so download anything you want to keep.

Photos are only sent anywhere if you self-host the app with an AI engine and choose that engine. They then go only to that provider.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| The floor or walls look bent or shifted | Re-fit the room box (step 3). Make sure the orange dot is inside the far wall. |
| Furniture blocks a doorway | The app looks for dark doorways and bright windows. If it missed one, it may place furniture there. Try a brighter photo, or move the room box slightly. |
| "This browser cannot create MP4 video" | Use Chrome, Edge or Safari, or the self-hosted version with ffmpeg installed. |
| The 3D view is blank or slow | Your browser needs WebGL. Try another browser and close other heavy tabs. |
| Projects disappeared | They are stored per browser and device. Check you are using the same browser, and that its data wasn't cleared. |
| AI engines are greyed out | They need the self-hosted version with an API key. See the [README](../README.md#option-2-run-it-yourself). |

Renders are visual previews, not construction plans. Check anything structural, electrical or plumbing with a qualified professional.
